OUR SCIENTIFIC APPROACHES

All Treatments

Discover the principal approaches used and investigated in regenerative medicine, from patient-derived cellular therapies to emerging cell-free technologies.

APPROACH 01

Autologous Cellular Therapeutics

Autologous cellular therapies use cells collected from the same patient who will receive them. Depending on the application, these cells may undergo processing before being administered.

Established examples include certain blood-forming stem cell transplants. Other autologous regenerative applications remain under clinical investigation.

Patient-Derived Cells
APPROACH 02

Allogeneic Cellular Therapeutics

Allogeneic therapies involve cells obtained from a donor rather than from the recipient. These cells may be used in established transplant procedures or investigated for new applications.

Donor compatibility, immune responses, cell processing, and long-term safety are important considerations in their development.

Donor-Derived Cells
APPROACH 03

Cell-Free Therapeutics

Cell-free regenerative research explores biological materials such as extracellular vesicles, secreted proteins, and other signaling molecules without administering living therapeutic cells.

These technologies are being studied for their biological effects. Many proposed regenerative applications, including exosome-based interventions, remain experimental.

Cell-Derived Biological Factors
APPROACH 04

Tissue Engineering & Biomaterials

Tissue engineering combines principles from cell biology, materials science, and biomedical engineering to develop structures that support tissue repair or replacement.

Researchers investigate scaffolds, engineered tissues, and biomaterials for applications ranging from wound management to more complex tissue reconstruction.

THE SCIENCE BEHIND REGENERATION

How Regenerative Medicine Works

Understanding the body's natural repair mechanisms and how emerging biomedical technologies investigate ways to support tissue restoration.

The human body contains specialized cells and biological signaling systems that help maintain tissues and respond to injury. Regenerative medicine builds on this biology to investigate approaches for repairing, replacing, or restoring damaged cells and tissues.

01

Cellular Renewal

Stem cells can self-renew and, under appropriate conditions, develop into specialized cell types. Researchers investigate how these properties may support tissue replacement and repair.

02

Biological Signaling

Cells communicate through signaling molecules that influence inflammation, healing, and tissue maintenance. Understanding these pathways is an important area of regenerative research.

03

Tissue Repair & Engineering

Tissue engineering investigates combinations of cells, biomaterials, and biological cues to create structures that may help repair or replace damaged tissue.

Regenerative medicine includes both established medical applications and experimental approaches that require further clinical evaluation.

THE PATH TO CLINICAL INNOVATION

From Research to Clinical Application

Developing regenerative therapies requires rigorous scientific investigation, safety evaluation, and clinical evidence before new approaches can become established medical treatments.

01
DISCOVERY

Scientific Discovery

Researchers investigate cell biology, tissue repair mechanisms, and potential therapeutic targets through laboratory studies and scientific research.

02
EVALUATION

Preclinical Evaluation

Promising approaches undergo laboratory testing and, where appropriate, preclinical studies to investigate biological activity, quality, and potential safety concerns.

03
CLINICAL RESEARCH

Clinical Trials

Authorized clinical studies evaluate safety, appropriate dosing or administration, and whether an investigational treatment provides meaningful benefits to patients.

04
CLINICAL TRANSLATION

Regulatory Review & Clinical Use

Regulatory authorities assess the available evidence and manufacturing standards before applicable approvals are granted. Approved therapies require ongoing safety monitoring.

Scientific progress requires evidence. Not every investigational approach advances through clinical trials or receives regulatory approval.
